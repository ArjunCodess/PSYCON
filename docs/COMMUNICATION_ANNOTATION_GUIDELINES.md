# Independent communication annotation guidelines

Version `communication-1`, exploratory operational definitions. These ratings describe a recorded session. They do not establish personality, occupation, intention, diagnosis, or psychological validity.

A reviewer listens to attributed audio and reads the surrounding exchange. Confirm the participant-to-speaker mapping before reviewing. Rate independently of A–T scores, model predictions, user-selected archetype goals, class labels, and other reviewers. Keep participant self-report as its own source type.

| Target | Observable definition | Opportunity and contextual evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| directness | An explicit claim or request whose intended content a listener can identify in the exchange. | An actual prompt, decision, claim, or request opportunity; hedging alone does not establish absence. |
| proposal_orientation | Concrete proposed actions or alternatives relevant to the task. | A task in which proposing options is appropriate; an assigned role supplies context, not a label. |
| questioning | Relevant information-seeking or clarification questions. | A context with a genuine information gap; rhetorical punctuation alone is insufficient. |
| acknowledgement | Explicit recognition of a peer's preceding contribution before responding. | A direct contribution with fair auditory and language access; agreement is not required. |
| topic_control | Initiating or redirecting a topic with a stated link and observable uptake by others. | Reviewed topic boundaries and others' responses; speaking time alone is insufficient. |

Use 0 for not observed despite fair opportunity, 1 for one weak/brief occurrence, 2 for repeated/noticeable behavior with limited effect, 3 for clear repeated behavior or an effect on the exchange, and 4 for sustained behavior or a strong effect. Use N/O for no fair opportunity or unsupported recording; leave an unprovided answer missing. These are project-defined ordinal categories, not validated scientific cutoffs.

Positive communication ratings need at least two separate contextual moments in the session. Record each start/end time, preceding event, observable response, effect, reviewer confidence 1–3, and validity categories. Do not make each supporting moment a separate training participant. Document overlap, opportunity, language access, moderation, quality, sensitive topics, and accommodations. Record an actual independent reviewer and retain disagreements. Adjudication cites the original answers and its rationale, never replaces them.

Download the versioned CSV templates from the application. The long format has one participant/item/reviewer per row and accepts structured evidence, metadata, context flags, validity checks, and human pattern summaries. Batch files require explicit session-local code mappings. A–T grouped summaries and guide-derived patterns remain separate from these independent communication labels.
