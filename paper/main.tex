\begin{abstract}
Behavior in a group discussion depends on the preceding exchange, speaking opportunity, and the participant's earlier behavior. PSYCON investigates whether speaker-specific conversational measurements can predict contextual observations reviewed by psychologists, and whether earlier personal history improves evidence-supported communication reports. The implemented research instrument normalizes recordings, diarizes speakers, transcribes speech, conservatively attributes words, and preserves timestamped exchanges. A versioned A--T marksheet distinguishes ordinal ratings from insufficient opportunity and missing answers. Reviewed labels can enter frozen participant/source-group datasets for per-item logistic classification; matched report conditions compare transcript-only, structured-context, and full-PSYCON representations. Independent rater agreement, held-out prediction error, and blinded report usefulness are the intended behavioral outcomes. These outcomes have not yet been established. Retained engineering artifacts cover eleven recordings and 85 marked person-recording slots. Guarded PSYCON produced 57 ready profiles, compared with 51 for each guarded Nemotron configuration; these are coverage counts, not identity accuracy or psychological validation. Ten scanned bundles contain 228 pages. Seventy-six imported version 5.0 forms yielded 750 numeric, 1,662 N/O, and 20 missing answers. A separate exploratory Docker fit retained 40 participant-session examples and fitted fourteen source-score targets using 423 real labels. All retained examples trained the models. Training agreement was 97.9\%; retrospective omitted-group agreement was 57.2\%, below a 64.4\% median baseline. Retrospective compact-model selection improved agreement to 62.2\% on the same labels, with MAE 0.490 versus the reference 0.460. Minority-score detection remained poor and independent behavioral validation is unavailable. Independent behavioral validation remains unavailable. The present contribution is an implemented measurement and evaluation workflow that keeps contextual human observations separate from model predictions. Its behavioral hypotheses remain open, and no diagnosis, stable personality inference, causal effect, or demonstrated coaching benefit is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Behavioral question and significance}
Two participants can have similar speaking time yet behave differently in a discussion. One may answer a direct question, another may respond to a different point; either may speak briefly because of turn allocation rather than disengagement. The behavioral problem is to describe what a person did in an identifiable exchange without turning a recording into a permanent judgment of that person.

PSYCON examines this problem through contextual psychologist observations linked to conversational evidence. Its principal question is: \emph{To what extent can speaker-specific talking patterns and discussion context predict contextual A--T ratings reviewed by school psychologists on held-out participants and recordings, and does adding strictly earlier personal history improve the support and usefulness of communication reports?}

The scientific object is behavior in context. Audio processing and supervised prediction are measurement tools. The current project is positioned in Behavioral and Social Sciences, with an emphasis on social interaction and operational behavioral observation. It is not a study of whether a software interface is attractive or whether a device can transmit packets. The Society for Science science-project criteria emphasize a testable question, appropriate variables and controls, systematic analysis, sufficient evidence, and understanding of limitations \cite{isefcriteria}.

The potential benefit is a reviewable account of participation, reciprocity, contribution structure, and responses to challenge. A participant or psychologist could inspect the exact moment supporting an observation, correct attribution, and see uncertainty. That benefit remains prospective. No study in the reviewed artifacts establishes improved well-being, learning, communication skill, or clinical outcomes.

\section{Psychological background and research gap}
\subsection{Interaction is sequential and context-dependent}
Conversation involves the organization of speaker turns, responses, and opportunities to enter. Foundational conversation-analysis work examines this organization rather than interpreting speaking duration as a complete description of social behavior \cite{sacks1974}. PSYCON uses that distinction operationally: a timing overlap is measurable, but deciding whether it was an intentional interruption requires the surrounding exchange and human review.

A response after disagreement is similarly ambiguous. Reduced participation could reflect being interrupted, a topic change, moderator allocation, language access, fatigue, or a deliberate decision to listen. The project therefore asks observers to describe an event, earlier behavior, a response, its duration, and its effect on the exchange. It does not infer an emotion from a pause or assign a trait from one session.

These operational choices do not constitute an established psychological theory of all communication. They narrow the question to behavior the source recording can support. A scientifically useful observation must be attributable to the correct person, defined reproducibly, and interpreted within an opportunity to exhibit it.

\subsection{Measurement reliability and construct validity}
Construct validity concerns whether evidence supports the interpretation of a measure, rather than whether a form merely has a psychological label \cite{cronbach1955}. The A--T groups and score meanings are project-defined. They have not been shown to form validated latent dimensions, to generalize across populations, or to measure anxiety, intelligence, diagnosis, or personality.

Independent observers are needed to establish whether the definitions can be applied reproducibly. Cohen's kappa measures chance-adjusted agreement, and weighted kappa can penalize ordinal differences differently \cite{cohen1960,cohen1968}. PSYCON preserves original ratings and adjudication separately so agreement can be evaluated before differences are resolved. High agreement would support reproducible use of the rubric in the studied setting, but would not establish every psychological interpretation attached to it.

The study also needs evidence that the recording and speaking opportunity support each score. A zero means the behavior was not observed despite a fair opportunity; N/O means opportunity or recording support was insufficient. Missing means no answer was supplied. Treating all three as zero would train on a changed target and could make poor recording access look like favorable behavior.

\subsection{What the project adds and what it does not}
Pretrained speech models provide transcription and diarization, including Whisper and Community-1 \cite{radford2022,community1}. They do not supply independent psychologist judgments of a participant's response to an exchange. PSYCON's contribution is the explicit connection between source evidence, reviewed human observations, annotation revisions, and independent evaluation units. The novelty claim is this project-specific measurement workflow, not that turn-taking or behavioral observation has never been studied.

The closest technical foundations answer different questions: Whisper transcribes speech; Community-1 separates anonymous speakers; TalkNet examines audiovisual speaking activity; and SpeechBrain provides speech-processing components \cite{tao2021,ravanelli2021}. Their published results cannot be transferred to A--T prediction. For the behavioral study, they are possible sources of measurement error and tools whose outputs require review.

Data leakage is another methodological concern. Shared participants, repeated clips, and derived recordings can make a nominally held-out result partly familiar to a model \cite{kapoor2022}. The study therefore groups dependent people and sources before splitting examples. This design choice matters because a clip-level success can otherwise be mistaken for generalization to a new person.

\section{Hypotheses, variables, and study design}
\subsection{Two separate behavioral evaluations}
Hypothesis H1 is that supported speaker-specific measurements and recorded interaction context predict some independently reviewed A--T item ratings with lower held-out mean absolute error than a constant training-median prediction. The null comparison is no improvement over that baseline. The hypothesis does not require every item to be predictable or every score to be equally frequent.

Hypothesis H2 is that richer report representations receive better independently reviewed evidence-grounding and usefulness judgments than a transcript-only representation, without increasing unsupported psychological claims. The current application compares transcript-only A, structured-context B, and full-PSYCON C. B and C share frozen supervised predictions when available. The report study is distinct from prediction: a model may agree with a rating while producing an unhelpful report, or a report may be useful without a fitted predictor.

The implemented C representation adds history, retrieved evidence, and exploratory reference information together. It cannot isolate a history-specific effect. A future matched ablation must vary history while holding evidence retrieval and references fixed before H2 can be narrowed to a causal claim about history. The application currently supports a package comparison, and the paper reports that boundary.

\subsection{Population, recruitment, and independence}
The intended human reference comes from school psychologists reviewing group-discussion behavior. The retained engineering corpus contains eleven recordings and 85 marked person-recording places. Author-supplied seat correspondences establish repeated people across seven recordings. Nineteen links merge the 85 slots into 66 provisional identity components, an upper bound on unique people rather than a confirmed sample size because unlisted overlaps remain unknown. The original coverage denominator remains 85 slots. Recruitment procedures, demographics, and an approved final behavioral sample size are not established by the benchmark.

The supplied mapping corrects left to right only in its first section. It links six right-indexed seats in the afternoon 1.15.33 WhatsApp recording to IMG\_3962; all eight same-position seats in the 10.19.44 and 10.19.02 recordings; one person across the 1.16.36 recording, IMG\_3997, and IMG\_3962; and three seats between 10.19.02 and IMG\_3983. Directions are relative to the displayed frame, not a diarization index. The companion \path{docs/research/PARTICIPANT_OVERLAP.json} preserves the exact pairs without publishing names or photographs.

These links connect the eleven recordings into six provisional recording components. Four recordings form one component and three form another; four remain singleton recordings. For unseen-participant evaluation, the recordings within each linked component must stay in the same partition. Additional participant or source links may merge components further, so six does not establish six independent groups. Author-supplied correspondence improves provenance but does not validate every word-to-speaker assignment or supply consent. No live person mappings or training partitions were changed by this documentation update.

The prospective behavioral study needs a documented sampling frame, voluntary recruitment, inclusion/exclusion rules, language access, relevant accommodations, and reviewed repeated-person mappings. A school convenience sample can support conclusions about the studied recordings, but cannot represent all adolescents or workplace communication. Moderator selection and topic assignment can also affect who gets a chance to display an item.

No formal power calculation is reported. The estimator's minimum data requirements are engineering gates, not a sample-size justification. Planning should use the desired precision of the primary paired outcome and the number of independent participant/source groups. Thousands of overlapping clips from a few discussions would not replace that independent sample size.

Scores range from 0 to 4. One means a weak or brief occurrence; two means repeated or noticeable behavior with limited effect; three means clear repeated behavior or an effect on the exchange; four means sustained behavior or a strong effect. The guide's interpretation thresholds are project rules, not scientifically calibrated cutoffs. N/O supports no conclusion about the absent observation, and missing answers remain missing.

A positive score requires contextual evidence. Item K and M--T need event and response windows; Q--T also need a baseline from earlier in the same discussion. For example, a reduced-participation label should include the challenge, earlier participation, later participation, and the opportunity to speak. Visible posture or gaze can be retained as human observation, but it cannot automatically train the audio-based predictor.

\subsection{Scanned material and the form-version problem}
Ten local scanned PDF bundles contain 228 image-only pages. The current import retains 76 participant forms from ten recordings as version 5.0, with A--T and P1--P12 role-related items. Across 2,432 answer records, 750 are numeric scores, 1,662 are N/O, and twenty are missing. The separately versioned source form is not silently renamed as the legacy version 4.0 rubric.

Imported transcriptions preserve their source version and answer states. Independent transcription review and rubric validation remain necessary; reviewer provenance is missing on 1,120 answer rows. Private scans, signatures, participant responses, and identifiable photographs are excluded from the paper and public figures.

Permission, ancestry, participant attribution, and independent label review remain separate gates. User-attested permission records are available, but item opportunity, observer confidence, structured evidence, validity checks, and independent reviews are absent. Forty-five imported mappings are stored as confirmed by historical model-supported seat transfer, seven are uncertain, and twenty-four are unmapped; stored confirmation is not independent human speaker validation. No answers meet strict behavioral eligibility. A separate source-score prototype retains those limitations explicitly.

\subsection{Review, eligibility, and unavailable targets}
The instrument stores observer answers and self-reports separately. It keeps original submissions, independent review, disagreements, adjudication, later corrections, and attachment provenance. Training requires documented permission, a current confirmed speaker mapping, source review, recording support, opportunity, observer confidence, and supported evidence. Reviewer repetitions are not additional independent examples.

Conflicting ratings require adjudication before supervised use. Original disagreement remains available for reliability analysis. Positive evidence must belong to the target speaker, event responses must follow the event, and same-session baselines must precede it. Missing and N/O answers are excluded from numeric fitting rather than imputed as favorable scores.

Item S remains excluded because supported vocal-change window predictors are absent from the current extractor. T may use supported participation change, while visual-only movement or gaze ratings remain stored and excluded from audio training. Independently defined communication targets have their own annotation requirements and are not generated from archetype preferences, A--T totals, or predicted answers.

\section{Procedure and software-supported measurement}
\subsection{Recording-to-evidence procedure}
The canonical instrument accepts supported audio/video files with original filename, recording time and timezone, context, anonymous participant codes, dataset role, and consent status. Originals retain their hashes and registered local paths. Exact hashes detect duplicate bytes, while reviewed source ancestry handles excerpts and re-encodings. Registration and queuing do not imply completed analysis.

The media worker records eight inspectable stages: preprocessing, diarization, transcription, attribution, segmentation, features, evidence, and profile. Audio is normalized to mono 16 kHz PCM with a playback copy separate from the original. Duration, clipping, silence, and corruption diagnostics can withhold unsupported analysis. An energy-contrast value is an SNR proxy, not a calibrated measure of recording noise. Aggressive denoising is disabled.

Community-1 creates anonymous intervals, and faster-whisper large-v3 provides timestamped transcription \cite{fasterwhisper}. The present transcription adapter fixes English, voice-activity filtering, beam size five, and disabled conditioning on previous text. These settings do not establish multilingual validity. The default limits are twelve speakers, four hours, and an 8 GiB upload per recording.

Word attribution requires strong interval coverage for one speaker and little competing coverage. Ambiguous overlapping words remain unattributed. Turn/utterance segmentation and neighboring exchanges create the behavioral context. A reviewer then connects a session participant to the correct anonymous cluster. Persistent person identity, session participant identity, and cluster identity remain distinct.

\subsection{Conversational measurements and denominators}
Speaking time is the union of a speaker's diarized intervals, including overlap. Speaking share uses exclusively attributed non-overlapping duration, $q_i=D_i^{\mathrm{exclusive}}/\sum_jD_j^{\mathrm{exclusive}}$. Turn count follows merging same-speaker intervals separated by at most 0.3 seconds. Words per minute divides attributed words by diarized speaking duration; incomplete attribution can lower this value, so it is not a complete measure of fluency.

Overlap-entry candidates require at least 0.5 seconds of entry and at least 0.2 seconds remaining in another speaker's turn. Their rate uses session duration and is expressed per ten minutes. This detects a temporal relation; interruption intent remains a human interpretation. Directed-response estimates use adjacent turns and bounded gaps, which can misidentify the intended addressee in a group.

Transparent English rules count question punctuation and selected proposal, hedging, acknowledgement, disagreement, reasoning, experimentation, and concession markers. Ratios use attributed utterances, while rates use session exposure. These are low-confidence proxies. Saying ``because'' does not establish reasoning quality, and punctuation does not independently determine the function of a question.

Manual event annotations can supply richer topic, argumentation, question-type, and turn-sharing descriptions. Their coverage is explicitly partial. Automatic topic control, persuasion, paraphrasing, and interruption intent remain unvalidated or unavailable. A missing semantic measure is not treated as proof that the behavior did not occur.

\subsection{Event change and personal history}
The Q--T baseline occurs before an identifiable event in the same session. Historical comparison instead asks how the current session differs from earlier conversations. Historical summaries use strictly earlier eligible completed recordings linked to a reviewed person, exclude reference-only material and related source duplicates, and restrict use by dataset role. One long recording does not gain extra weight simply because it contains more windows.

The implementation retains recording-level means, medians, variance, quantiles, sample counts, and source lineage. Availability requires five comparable prior recordings. A difference exceeding two sample standard deviations is a descriptive prototype flag, not a significance test, and five recordings do not establish stable personality. Neither same-session change nor historical deviation shows that a challenge caused the observed response.

\section{Analysis plan and implemented predictor}
\subsection{Independent units and frozen datasets}
The snapshot builder retains labels, mappings, source hashes, consent and review records, feature versions, and partition assignments. The example unit is a participant in a session, with separate supported item targets. Connected components join recordings through confirmed repeated people and shared/reviewed original sources, and cannot cross unseen-participant train/validation/test boundaries. Reference overlap and incompatible roles are excluded.

This grouping may connect much of a small corpus, leaving too few independent groups to fit. Randomly splitting clips would hide that limitation rather than solve it. A separate longitudinal protocol permits repeat-person evaluation with explicit training/validation/evaluation roles and strict forward-time cutoffs. Unseen-person generalization and within-person prediction are different estimands and require separate reports.

The current supervised snapshot uses conversational measurements, context indicators, transcript confidence, and supported target-specific event-window features. Historical report summaries are not automatically inserted as predictor inputs. A prediction claim about personal history therefore needs an additional explicit feature experiment. This separation prevents a feature that only exists in reporting from being described as trained supervision.

\subsection{Estimator, baseline, and selection}
Supported targets use median imputation with missingness indicators, scaling, and class-balanced logistic regression from scikit-learn \cite{pedregosa2011}. The estimator approximates ordinal 0--4 scores as categorical classes; it does not enforce an ordinal threshold structure. Default regularization candidates are $C\in\{0.1,1,10\}$, selected by validation mean absolute error. Preprocessing fits on training data, and final test examples do not select the configuration.

At least twelve training examples, three independent training groups, two score classes, and supported measured predictors are required. These minimums are software gates, not evidence of statistical power or adequate population coverage. If validation is absent, the implementation avoids hyperparameter search and marks unsupported evaluation as exploratory.

The constant comparator predicts the integer training median. The portable artifact retains feature order, medians, missingness indicators, scaling, classes, coefficients, and intercepts, and its predictions are checked against the fitted estimator. A model remains a candidate until deliberately activated. Predictions retain model/input lineage, and correction or withdrawal identifies dependent artifacts requiring invalidation or retirement.

\subsection{Statistical outcomes and uncertainty}
For each target, the primary prediction outcome is $\mathrm{MAE}=n^{-1}\sum_r|y_r-\widehat y_r|$. The code also records macro-F1, balanced accuracy, a five-class confusion matrix, quadratic-weighted kappa, and multiclass Brier score. Probabilities are uncalibrated and cannot be presented as validated confidence. A 500-draw connected-group bootstrap supplies an MAE interval when enough independent held-out groups exist.

The software's ``evaluated'' gate requires minimum validation/test counts and MAE below the constant baseline in both partitions. This status is not a statistical significance test, a validity certification, or a deployment approval. Coverage computed on retained examples is not coverage of all stored sheets. Final analysis must report exclusions, N/O, missingness, abstention, class distributions, participant counts, independent-source counts, and uncertainty.

The prospective study should predeclare one primary target or aggregate outcome justified by the behavioral question, its analysis unit, and the meaningful improvement. Per-item secondary comparisons need a multiplicity policy. Rater reliability should be assessed before adjudication, and small or constant-score samples should be reported without forcing a kappa estimate. No p-values or confidence intervals for human outcomes are invented in this paper.

\subsection{Blinded report comparison}
A uses the first forty selected utterances around the target speaker's first contribution, including neighboring speakers for context. B adds session measurements, interaction context, and frozen predictions when available. C adds earlier history, retrieved speaker evidence, and exploratory references. The same transcript window is used in all three conditions; it is not a full-recording interpretation.

The local Ollama model's actual digest, prompts, inputs, output, attempts, and decoding settings are stored. Defaults use temperature zero, seed 42, a 16,384-token context setting, and 3,200 generated tokens. At most 24 retrieved evidence records are selected across indicator types. Invalid, wrong-speaker, oversized, and truncated outputs are withheld rather than substituted with invented claims.

Evidence-ID validation checks reference integrity, not semantic support. Independent reviewers must play the cited source and judge whether the claim follows. Planned primary review dimensions are grounding, correct attribution, contextual appropriateness, usefulness, and overclaiming. Exploratory role-reference agreement is secondary. Randomized condition order, masked system labels, common source material, and retained failed runs are necessary controls; no completed blinded behavioral outcome is reported.

\section{Current results and evidential boundaries}
\subsection{Behavioral outcomes remain pending}
The release does not supply independently validated held-out A--T prediction results, psychologist inter-rater agreement, blinded usefulness scores, or longitudinal improvement estimates. Imported raw scores support an exploratory computational fit, not those outcomes. H1 and H2 remain untested in the reported human data.

This boundary is the main behavioral finding of the current evidence audit: the instrument supports a testable procedure, but the available artifacts do not yet answer the behavioral question. A software demonstration is not an effect size about people. The evidence does support reporting recorded engineering comparisons and explaining the remaining measurement gates.

\subsection{Retrospective omitted-group diagnostics}
After the final fit, a separate exploratory analysis left out one of five known person/source groups at a time and refitted the same fixed-$C$ estimator on the others. Feature availability, imputation, scaling, and median baselines were derived from each fold's training data only. Original fitting gates were reapplied; inadequate folds abstained. This retrospective analysis was not preregistered and cannot establish an independent prospective final-test result. Source ratings remain unreviewed, and unresolved overlaps may still permit leakage.

The deployed artifacts reproduced 414 of 423 training labels exactly (97.9\%), with mean absolute error 0.026 score units. Omitted-group models scored 402 labels, abstained on twenty-one, and matched 230 exactly (57.2\%). On the same scored labels, fold training-median baselines achieved 64.4\% agreement. Model MAE was 0.562 versus baseline MAE 0.460. Thus the pooled raw-score model underperformed this simple baseline. P1--P12 supplied 397 scored observations; G and I supplied only three and two. This chiefly measures role/task source-score approximation, not comprehensive A--T generalization. The gap is consistent with overfitting or group differences, but does not identify their cause or formally settle H1.

\subsection{Retrospective model repair and class sensitivity}
A subsequent development analysis compared nine disclosed candidates: training median and mode references, logistic classifiers at $C=0.01,0.1,1$ without class balancing and $C=0.01,0.1$ with balancing, and rounded ridge predictions at $\alpha=10,100$. Each outer fold omitted one known connected group. Selection used only inner omitted-group folds from the remaining groups, minimizing equal-group MAE within each target; preprocessing and feature availability were derived from the relevant training fold. A candidate needed an inner MAE improvement of at least 0.05 over the median reference, otherwise the reference was retained. The original count/group/class fitting gates applied to feature-based fits; inadequate fits used an explicitly labelled training-median reference. This policy was developed after inspecting the earlier results, so nested splitting does not make it preregistered or an untouched independent test.

On the same 402 item observations evaluated previously, nested selection achieved 60.9\% exact agreement and MAE 0.493, compared with original 57.2\%/0.562 and median-reference 64.4\%/0.460. The 3.7-point accuracy gain and approximately 12.4\% error reduction did not establish superiority to baseline. Mean sensitivity over observed score classes decreased from 30.3\% to 28.8\%; median-reference sensitivity was 29.6\%. Sensitivity is exact-score recall, not clinical sensitivity or psychological validity. Original recalls for scores 1--4 were 15.4\%, 0\%, 33.0\%, and 72.9\%; revised recalls were 15.4\%, 0\%, 13.6\%, and 86.1\%, on respective supports 13, 20, 103, and 266. Score 0 had no observations, so its sensitivity is unavailable. Pooled targets have different behavioral meanings and do not constitute one latent construct.

Including reference fallbacks, the revised procedure covered all 423 labels with 60.0\% agreement and MAE 0.508, versus median-reference 63.4\%/0.478. A paired bootstrap resampling five known groups produced a descriptive 95\% percentile interval of 0.009--0.052 score units for model-minus-reference MAE; few groups, unresolved overlaps and retrospective development prevent a population or significance claim. Final fitting selected nine median references and five feature-based estimators. Fourteen portable fits were registered as exploratory candidates and replayed against all 423 training labels, with 77.3\% training agreement. They were not promoted because the evaluated selection procedure remained inferior to baseline. All attempted results, class supports, precision, specificity and fold choices remain inspectable.

\subsection{Compact predictors and ordinal modeling}
A further disclosed development procedure addressed the mismatch between 31 available predictors and forty participant-session examples. Four predefined core measurements were speaking share, words per minute, mean turn duration and response latency. A second set added question rate, proposal rate, acknowledgement ratio and interruption-candidate rate. These remain automated measurements or rule-based proxies, not validated psychological constructs. Availability was determined only within the relevant training fold, with no identity, topic or other-target response features added.

Twelve fixed candidates comprised median/mode references, core logistic models at $C=0.1,1$, compact logistic models at $C=0.1$ with and without class balancing, core/compact ridge at $\alpha=10$, compact cumulative-threshold logistic models at $C=0.1,1$, and compact random-forest/extra-tree regressors. Ensembles used 64 trees, depth at most three and at least four examples per leaf. Ordinal fits independently estimated $P(Y>k)$ for $k=0,1,2,3$; cumulative-minimum projection enforced nonincreasing exceedance probabilities, followed by a lower-median ordinal decision. These probabilities are uncalibrated. Regression produced rounded bounded scores without invented class probabilities. Logistic ties within $10^{-12}$ probability units preferred the lower class, while older artifacts retained their previous tie behavior.

Inner selection minimized equal-group MAE among candidates whose pooled observed-class sensitivity was no more than 0.02 below the median reference, and required a 0.025 MAE gain over that reference. Otherwise it retained the reference. The twelve-example/three-known-group/two-class fitting gates and training-only preprocessing remained in force. This sensitivity constraint governs inner selection; it cannot guarantee recall in an omitted outer group. The policy was fixed before this particular run, after previous results had been examined, and therefore is retrospective rather than preregistered.

The compact procedure achieved 250/402 exact matches (62.2\%), MAE 0.490 and mean class sensitivity 30.0\% on the common comparison labels. This is a 5.0-percentage-point agreement increase and approximately 12.8\% lower error relative to the original model, but it remains worse than median-reference 64.4\%/0.460 in agreement/error. Exact-score recalls for scores 1--4 were 15.4\%, 0\%, 18.4\% and 86.1\%, with supports 13, 20, 103 and 266. No reliable minority-score detection or broad behavioral validity follows.

Across all 423 labels, including reference fallbacks, compact selection produced 61.2\% agreement and MAE 0.506 versus median-reference 63.4\%/0.478. A paired five-known-group bootstrap yielded a descriptive model-minus-reference MAE interval of $[-0.013,0.049]$, spanning zero. Few groups, unresolved overlap and prior examination prevent a superiority or population claim. Final fitting produced eight population references and six feature-based models, with training replay agreement 73.5\%. Fourteen artifacts were saved as exploratory candidates without promotion. All twelve fixed-candidate outer diagnostics were retained; choosing their apparent winner would add selection bias. For example, mode-reference agreement was 64.5\% over all 423 labels, further emphasizing the need for multiple simple controls.

\section{Interpretation, alternative explanations, and limitations}
The main threat is interpreting a conversational proxy as a psychological construct. Low word count can result from moderation or limited access; overlap can be normal cooperation; a marker such as ``I agree'' need not be sincere acknowledgement. The study must evaluate the actual operational item against source evidence instead of assuming that a plausible feature name measures the intended behavior.

The strongest alternative explanation for future predictor agreement is shared context or observer practice. A model could learn which topic, group, moderator, or recording quality tends to attract certain scores instead of learning a participant's behavior. Group/source separation, context-stratified errors, independent observers, and feature ablations can expose this. A null or reversed improvement would challenge H1 and should be reported directly.

Another alternative is common-source error. Observers and the model could both rely on an incorrect transcript or cluster mapping. Reviewers should have access to original audio, and attribution error must be measured independently rather than inferred from rating agreement. Removing uncertain examples can improve conditional performance while reducing real-world coverage, so exclusion and abstention require equal visibility.

For reports, C may look better because it has more evidence or more polished information, rather than because earlier history is informative. The history-only ablation is the strongest single follow-up to isolate that interpretation. A report that receives higher usefulness ratings but more unsupported personal claims would not satisfy the intended benefit. Familiarity with a participant or inferred condition identity could also bias reviewers.

Human data remain limited by unresolved source-version differences, absent confirmed recruitment/sample-size information, and pending independent evaluation. A school convenience sample, fixed English processing, limited language access, unequal opportunities, and unvalidated semantic rules limit generalization. Current ordinal approximation and rare score classes further constrain estimator interpretation. Stable personality and causal effects are outside the evidence.

Practical problems included overlapping speech, short clean turns, ambiguous identity, slow remote-database reads, GPU contention, and preserving corrections during retries. Conservative attribution, durable jobs, adapter boundaries, grouped queries, and immutable revisions address operation, but do not remove psychological measurement limitations. Local single-user execution and incomplete physical-phone/media-backup verification also limit product readiness.

\section{Future studies and potential impact}
The next behavioral study should review the versioned 5.0 transcriptions and rubric crosswalk, verify permission and mapping independently, collect source-supported opportunity/confidence and independent ratings, and freeze an adequately sized participant/source split. It should report reproducibility of human scoring before model performance. If an item lacks agreement or adequate opportunity, the appropriate response is to revise or exclude it transparently.

The most informative report follow-up is a paired history-only experiment with the same speaker, transcript, evidence retrieval, reference information, local model, and decoding settings. Independent raters would judge support, usefulness, and overclaiming from source playback. The comparison can challenge the claim that personal history helps; collecting a larger sample without isolating these inputs would not resolve that mechanism.

Longer-term work could add reviewed semantic annotations, supported vocal-change windows, ordinal estimators, and calibration, with new feature versions and held-out comparison. The envisioned product is voluntary communication review that lets a person inspect and correct evidence and practice a specific adjustment. Demonstrated benefit in the studied population must precede wider school or workplace claims. Diagnosis, recruitment ranking, and disciplinary scoring are not supported uses.

Hardware remains a secondary future branch. Retained designs describe ESP32 wrist/audio modules, physiological sensors, an I2S microphone, timestamps, and BLE. Ear firmware contains I2S capture and protocol transport; wrist sensor packets still use placeholders. No tracked KiCad schematic, PCB, or validated manufactured assembly exists. A future three-dimensional assembly diagram, KiCad circuit/board, verified power protection, pin assignments, bill of materials, and bench records would prepare a separate approved synchronized-sensor study. They do not supply current behavioral results.

\section{Conclusion}
PSYCON provides an implemented pathway for studying contextual behavior with source-linked psychologist observations, distinct opportunity states, reviewed attribution, and independent evaluation boundaries.
